01 · Why this exists

Your personalization is a tell.

Most “AI email” is not writing. It is a merge field wearing a compliment. The recipient already knows the pattern: you exported a title, a company, and a first name from LinkedIn, poured it into a model, and asked the model to sound warm.

Warmth from a stranger who has never met you is not warmth. It is a costume. People who get a lot of inbound can clock the costume in one sentence. They do not mark it as spam because they are polite, or busy, or the filter missed it. They delete it with their eyes.

The test: take out the first name, the company, and the job title. If nothing specific remains, you did not personalize. You labeled.

What harvested + generated looks like from the other side

  • A compliment that could apply to anyone with a pulse and a profile.
  • A “reason for reaching out” that is just their industry restated.
  • A request for 15 minutes that arrives before a reason to care.
  • Rhythm that is too even: three-part lists, balanced clauses, no sharp edges.
  • A sign-off from a first name that does not match the sending domain, or matches it too perfectly, like a brand that was born last Tuesday.

If you are automating, you are allowed to be brief and commercial. You are not allowed to pretend you have a relationship. That is the whole book.

03 · The rules

Write like a person who is busy.

  1. If you did not look, do not pretend. “Saw you posted about X” is allowed only when you can quote the actual point. Otherwise skip the compliment.
  2. One line of context that could only be them. A warehouse constraint. A shipping lane. A public complaint. A job they opened. Not “your work in the space.”
  3. One ask that fits in a reply. “Want the 90-second clip?” beats “find time on my calendar.” Meetings are a second step, earned.
  4. Cut the throat-clearing. Delete the first two sentences. Start at the third. If the email still works, the first two were slop.
  5. Sound like Slack, then add one comma of politeness. If you would not say it to a peer you respect, do not put it in Times New Roman and call it enterprise.
  6. Name the company and the reason in sentence one or two. Mystery is not intrigue when you are uninvited.
  7. Subject lines should be specific and slightly boring. “Pallet labels / 1-hour waste” beats “Quick idea for {{Company}}!” Clever is how spam filters stay employed.
  8. Stop after two follow-ups unless they replied. Persistence is not a personality. It is a setting.
Automation rule: you may merge facts you actually have. You may not invent familiarity. A true trigger (“you downloaded the rate card,” “you asked support about carton sizes”) is personalization. A generated compliment is fraud with better punctuation.

04 · Before / after

Same goal. One of these can be read.

Before — harvested, generated, and proud of it

Subject: Quick question, {{FirstName}} Hi {{FirstName}}, I came across your profile and was really impressed by your work in the industry. As a fellow innovator, I’d love to explore how we can leverage synergies and unlock growth together. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute chat next week? Best, A prompt

After — a human with a reason

Subject: Pallet labels eating an hour a day Hi Maya, You mentioned last month that bay labels were slowing the morning pick. We print from the same scan the picker already does. If that’s still a mess, I can send a 90-second clip. No call. Tom Unslop Press

What changed

  • The subject names a problem, not a vibe.
  • The first line refers to a thing only she said, or a thing only her operation has.
  • The ask is a file, not a meeting.
  • No journey. No fellow. No synergies. No hope that the email finds her well. It found her. That is enough.

If you cannot write the “after” without stalking them, you do not have a reason to email them. That is not a copy problem. That is a list problem.

05 · The checklist

Twenty seconds before send.

Fail two and you are not doing email marketing. You are doing cosplay.

#QuestionIf no
1Can I point to a fact that is not their name, title, or company?Do not invent one. Shorten the email.
2Did I remove every sentence I would be embarrassed to read aloud?Read it aloud. Delete the costume.
3Is the ask answerable in one reply?Downgrade the ask.
4Would this still make sense if I hid the first name?You labeled, you did not write.
5Did I name who I am and why I am allowed to bother them?Add it. Mystery is not a strategy.
6Did I avoid hope / journey / fellow / leverage / 15 minutes?You already know what to cut.
7If this is automated, is every merged token a real fact?Turn the “personal” line off.
8Is there a real way to say no or never hear from me again?Put one in. Adults offer exits.
